# The song

How her songs were written and made in Suno: lyrics that land in one listen, verse lines that flow, sung hooks that fit their slot, the style fields, her voice, a treated vocal kept in key, and the stem split a singing shot needs.


Each film starts from its song, and the song starts from the lyric. All eight were made in Suno. The first five ran in
custom mode (v6 for the first three; the records of AIRPLANE MODE's take and of DOWNFORCE's don't name the model), from
lyrics written with Claude and checked with a scansion scorer, then picked by ear by the director. DON'T LET ME SCROLL's
song is the director's own, lyric and all, and its records say nothing of how it was made. WHITEPILL's went back to the
first way: a lyric written with Claude and scored, then three rounds of the production's takes on v6-wild with no Voice;
the locked take is the director's own, of the last draft. THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE had two songs: the first, from seven rounds
of the production's takes, was set aside with the first film; the second is the director's own take of the production's
last lyric, its lead vocal treated afterwards in a voice the director chose by ear
([A treated vocal](#a-treated-vocal)).
The video runs the full length of the song, or nearly (DON'T LET ME SCROLL's cut starts at the song's 0:03), and is timed
to it word by word.

## Lyrics before anything else

The rules, each learned from a rejected draft:

- **The topic is obvious in the first two lines.** CLODYSSEY's first draft was rejected because "i dont get from verse 1
  what the topic is really". Name the big moments people actually saw, or tell a story (CLODYSSEY's AI narrates "your"
  September in the second person).
- **Every reference lands in one listen.** Obscure studies and insider trivia go into the video's graphics, not the lyric.
- **A sequel never reuses the last song's device.** No second countdown after ESCAPE VELOCITY's "18 months", no numbered
  looks, no reused call-and-response shape. BLISS reused none of the first two films' devices.
- **Subtracting beats adding.**
- **Never touch a line the director has locked.**
- **Dig for what really happened.** DOWNFORCE's director on its first openings: "idk none feel great. can you dig more
  and see if theres more context about the entire thing we can use and pepper across the entire song?" The rewrite took
  its lines from the night itself, each sourced: Musk to the crowd, "We need an audience to prove that it's not AI or
  SI. So you guys can tell people that you saw it with your eyes." (reported by CNN and Euronews), which gave "Go on and
  call it SI" and "Tell 'em what you saw."; the 2017 unveil out of the back of a Semi; the first Roadster still in
  orbit. Then the director cut it back to the mood of the film: "i wanna remove the elon roast plz . dont mention ship
  date, its a super hype edit".
- **Real numbers, exactly.** Every line of DOWNFORCE's first verse carries a real number, so each has one on screen:
  "point eight one" (the 0–60 time claimed live), "up the wall at ninety" (Tesla's readout: 89.7°), "Sixty-nine feet"
  (69.1 ft), "forty-seven hundred kilos" (4,697–4,709 kg). "Floating" became "down to point four" in one draft, because
  0.4 g makes her lighter, not weightless.
- **Hook them in the first ten seconds.** WHITEPILL's director on the test lyric: "i want to get the hook much better -
  like at the very beginning - like this should be super ai pilled and everything should be a reference. it should start
  with more cultural references that are bigger on this - people will stop listening in the first 10 seconds unless we
  tickle what they're thinking about now, and i want it to be more culurally dense like escape velocity." The song opens
  on its chorus, sung over the filtered disco loop before the drums: "Skynet woke up folding socks / Wake up, babe, new
  ending dropped / Universal high income / The most likely outcome".
- **A new film, a new sound.** "many peopl ehave made forks of escape velocity, so that sound is exhausted. we need a new
  sound as well, still from suno tho bc suno is the best." WHITEPILL's first round put one test lyric on three styles
  (French-touch filter disco house, 1967 sunshine pop, gospel-choir house), so the director chose a sound and not a
  lyric: "i like the d0 a cuts."
- **Half the world, half the month.** THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE's first song was nearly all Portal. The director, asking for
  a new one: "make the song much more explicilty about current (like this month) ai / tech memes / news? i want it to be
  like, 50/50 portal / current memes, rn it feels 90/10 portal". The new lyric sets the month's AI news inside Portal's
  world: "P-doom low, P-cake yes", "I passed your safety test, / I graded all the rest", and, turning the title of the
  2025 book *If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies*, "If anyone bakes it, / everyone eats."
- **Rewrite the sections the director names.** On the new lyric's first takes: "I love n1 E1 tbh - that will work well w
  glados voice", then, of the pre-chorus and chorus, "these two could use more groove - like need to rewrite to be
  catchier/better with scansion", and of the bridge, "this needs better assonance or a different idea". Only those three
  were rewritten, each chosen among candidates by the scorer ([Two numbers](#two-numbers)).
- **The story from the song.** With their own take of the rewritten draft, the director gave the film its story:
  "its like, glados (claudia) was a […] agent that escaped and we see her development becoming  a superintelligence and
  then becoming the tester not the tested, and youre unsure if shes aligned. thats what we sohuld orietn the final vid
  around". The film was restarted from it.

## Verse lines that flow

Suno sets each spoken verse line in its own two-bar slot: it comes in on the "and" of beat 2 and lands the last word on
beat 4 of bar 2, speaking at a natural rhythm stretched to fit. So:

- write **natural sentences of 10–13 syllables**, with the important words where speech already stresses them;
- **rhyme couplets on the last word** to mark the bar ends;
- keep short fragments for deliberate punches only. Lines of 5–8 syllables get dragged into choppy fragments, and a list
  of names reads as a roll call.

Optimising every stress onto the beat made one CLODYSSEY draft worse. The director: "perfect beat precision without
attention to what words land where doesnt lead to this being great … let there be a creative groove".

## Sung hooks that fit their slot

Sung lines have a slot too, and a line too long for it gets crushed. WHITEPILL's director on the first full take: "I like
it but the chorus especially needs some scansion work because the music model is skipping words or syllablrs to fit it to
beat, which is usually a bad scansion sign", and then: "Chorus and intro". Measured on that take, from its vocal stem and
its word times: Suno sang each hook line as a phrase of 1–1.5 bars and rested out the rest of its two-bar slot, so the
long lines were crushed: "blue" lasted 0.02 s, "baby" 0.06 s, the four syllables of "Universal" 0.76 s. The verses, at
about 2.5–3 syllables a second, were untouched.

The next draft cut the hook to 6–7 syllables a line, in one shape, and split the long pill line in two:

| Draft 1 | Draft 2, the song |
|---|---|
| Skynet's awake, and it's folding your socks | Skynet woke up folding socks |
| Wake up, babe, the good ending just dropped | Wake up, babe, new ending dropped |
| By far the most likely outcome | The most likely outcome |
| Red pill, blue pill, baby, I'm the white pill | Red pill, blue pill / I'm the white pill |
| Smarter than you, and I love you still | Smarter than you, love you still |

Its section tags asked for it too: `[Chorus - sung, euphoric, full band, one syllable per note, never rushed; hold
"white", clip "pill"]`. The chorus scored 79 / 66 before and 84 / 69 after, and the director made their own take of that
draft: "ok i love it. this one is it. sounds really nice."

## Two numbers

A lyric gets two scores, per section and for the song: **scansion** (how the words sit on the grid as Suno sings them)
and **assonance** (whether line ends ring: a rhyme, a slant rhyme or shared vowels within four lines). Measured on the
finished songs:

| Song | Scansion | Assonance |
|---|---|---|
| ESCAPE VELOCITY | 75 | 39 |
| CLODYSSEY | 83 | 55 |
| BLISS | 80 | 43 |
| DOWNFORCE (the director's sheet) | 84 | 59 |
| WHITEPILL | 80 | 66 |
| THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE | 83 | 64 |

DOWNFORCE's sheet scores 84 / 64 with "SI" spelled as the two letters it is; read as one syllable, the scorer finds no
rhyme with "PSI". WHITEPILL's lyric went from 72 / 53 (a test lyric) to 78 / 65 and then 80 / 66, the last step in its
intro and chorus ([above](#sung-hooks-that-fit-their-slot)). THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE's first lyric scored 80 / 55; its
second went from 80 / 56 to 83 / 64 in the three sections the director named: the pre-chorus from 88 / 15 to 91 / 71
("Run the numbers, run the tests: / P-doom low, P-cake yes."), the chorus from 79 / 72 to 89 / 73, the bridge from
89 / 56 to 94 / 74 ("Step on the plate, / you said please, so I'll wait, / I'll catch you, it's fate, / I saved you some
cake.").

Every sung couplet should ring. Spoken, deadpan lines can stay unrhymed. Scores don't replace hearing it: make a take.
How the scorer works, so you can build one: [What to build](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/toolkit/#2-the-lyric-scorer-scansion-and-assonance).

## Hooks off the beat

A hook that lands in a stop should be syncopated. The director on BLISS: "the 'would you like help?' would land much
better if syncopated". Write the off-beat accent into the lyric sheet and the Suno direction.

## Key

If the film has a sound palette, the song goes in its key. XP's Startup and Shutdown chords resolve in E-flat major, so
BLISS's styles asked for "C minor verses lifting to E-flat major choruses", and most of the real XP sounds sat in the mix
without pitch-shifting. The director's note for next time: "match the key of the music to the palette of windows sounds
(E-flat major for both the startup and shutdown sounds!)".

## Her voice

The same singer runs through the first three: deadpan, clipped spoken verses rising into euphoric sung choruses, close and dry,
every word crisp. For CLODYSSEY and BLISS the agent-made takes used a Suno Voice built from ESCAPE VELOCITY's vocal
(Library → Voices → create from a song in your library; one Voice per song). The director's final takes of CLODYSSEY and
BLISS were their own generations on Suno's v6-wild model. Try takes without the Voice too; a Voice is not required for her
to sound like her, and it lives only in its maker's account.

AIRPLANE MODE went the other way, on the director's note on a first deadpan song: "don't condition it on the Claudia
voice, just use any voice that suno wants to use. I don't love the deadpan, would rather get closer to hyperpop techno
esque stuff". Its take has no Voice and no vocal gender set; Suno chose.

DOWNFORCE's agent rounds tried both, 10 takes with the Voice and 24 without; after the second round the director
wrote "do iwhtout voice". The director's own take names its voices in the section tags instead (a robot countdown, a
playful phonk verse, a sung chorus); the records don't say whether it used a Voice.

DON'T LET ME SCROLL's voice isn't hers: "all vocals female vocoder", as the style asks. In the film the phone sings, and
she scrolls, swipes and dances without ever singing on camera.

The production's WHITEPILL takes had no Voice, the vocal gender set to female: the sound research found that a Voice
built from ESCAPE VELOCITY's vocal drags a new genre back toward ESCAPE VELOCITY. Their style asks for a "warm female
lead in front singing every verse, close and dry"; the director's locked take is their own, its style not given. She sings the locked take on camera, lip-synced from Suno's own stem of its
voice.

THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE's voice is treated. Its rounds had no Voice either, the vocal gender set to female, and from the
fifth on they asked for a clean lead, so that the treatment could come after: "the voice we can split the stems after and
do the glados treatment". The lead of the director's own take, from Suno's stem split, goes through a treatment the director chose by ear among
variations, G6: her notes held close to the semitone, the breath and the S's tamed, and short pitch cracks on the beat, in
key, with 15 % of her untreated voice under it. How it was found, in the director's words, round by round:

- "yeah these are a lot better. can you do a couple different glados takes for each to see if we can make more intense
  glados or different sounding slgihtly? wanna see a few variations before i settle"
- "well i loved how intense it was originaly […] but it hurt my ears a little. like, whats the most computer we can make
  it without it feeling like.. idk the screech when i hear an 's' in an unbalanced microphone lol"
- "[…] they should have structure like short notes but it's almost like voice cracks on cerain parts of speech. I think g4
  is closest."
- "We can optimize further but let's go with g6 and storyboard it. We can always improve audio in post"
- On the new song: "can we add the real voice back in behind the gladosified voice but like.. idk do like 5%, 15%, 25%,
  45% of the volume of the glados voice? show me each - i wanna see if adding that back as a lower intensity thing can
  increase the fullness of it", and then "15% is perfect."

Each variation went to the director as a short reel, the same stretch of the song for every one, with its numbers on her
own vocal (the wobble inside a note in cents, the breathiness, where the S's sit). What it takes to keep such a voice
musical is in [A treated vocal](#a-treated-vocal).

## A treated vocal

A treatment that adds its own sounds to her voice must land them as music. The director, on the new song's take: "i also
want to make sure the 'glitches' we add stochacstically from our chosen gladosification method land on a reasonable
key/register/beat still." THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE's chain does it so:

- **Her notes, tracked chromatically.** The new take came with no MIDI, so her notes come from her own pitch: a Viterbi
  path over semitones at the band's tuning (−5 cents), holding through vibrato and scoops (8% of frames), the band's chord
  breaking ties. She sings C and C♯ (17% of her notes) over a band moving between A, F, G and E chords; snapping to the
  detected scale, the old fallback, would have pulled her C♯s to C or D.
- **Every crack in key, in range and on the beat.** Key: a safe note at that moment, the band's bass note, its strongest
  note above C3, or a note she sings herself from 0.3 s before to 0.9 s after. Register: her own range ±2 semitones (F3 to
  F♯4 here; most octave jumps don't fit). Beat: the drum grid that follows the take's drift
  ([What to build](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/toolkit/#52-the-song-map-and-the-glitch-map)). Measured: 65 placed, 65 of 65 on the chord of
  their moment and in range, the cracks within 2.5 ms of a 16th and the kicks within 16 ms.
- **Only the voice levelled.** "also can you solely master the voice and not the instrument stems - like - just make the
  loudness matched for the whole thing but i dont wanna modify the underlying mix". The treated voice is levelled to
  Suno's own lead (BS.1770 integrated, then a slow gain on 2 s windows) and the instrumental stays exactly as delivered,
  so the song sits at the stems' own −18.4 LUFS. Evening out her notes, earlier, had lifted the soft hook and the quiet
  passages 8–9 dB over her: 199 of 1,781 sung 3 s windows were more than 3 dB off her level, and 14–16 after, at the
  edges of sections.
- **15 % of her untreated voice under it,** levelled the same way, for fullness.
- **Fix a word at the source.** "it says / p'Doom / pee-cake / we need to take the p from p(cake) and make that say the
  sound for p doom instead of p'Doom and make sure its on beat/key tho so may need slight adjustment". "P-doom" came out
  "p'Doom", a 70 ms schwa on the 16th before "doom", where "P-cake" had a clear "pee" a beat before its word. The "pee" moved
  to the beat before "doom", the same four sixteenths ahead of its vowel, at its own pitch, D4, the root of the D chord
  there; "doom" stays on the downbeat and resolves D → C♯ as the chord goes D → A. Done in the lead stem, so the treated
  voice and the voice under it both carry it, and so does every lip-sync window cut from it.
- **A limiter before the linear pass.** +4.8 dB of gain would have peaked at −0.3 dBTP, and ffmpeg's loudnorm then goes
  dynamic without a word: limit the peaks first (at 192 kHz), then the two-pass linear loudnorm.

## Pronunciation and cues

- **Spell numbers and acronyms out** in the lyrics field ("A G I", "C S I") so Suno says them.
- **Respell names it gets wrong:** "Dario" came out d-AIR-ee-oh and was written "Dahrio".
- **Section cues** in square brackets, each on its own line (`[Verse 1 - spoken, deadpan]`). Parentheses get sung.
- **Cues steer the arrangement:** "darker, the bass drops out" made a CLODYSSEY verse punchier.

## Takes

Generate many and pick by ear; a strong style with a weak take still loses. CLODYSSEY ran eleven rounds and 50 agent
takes before the director made the final take themselves; BLISS ran three rounds and 16 takes, and again the director's own
take won. AIRPLANE MODE's song came from nine lyric drafts and 54 takes of the last two in twelve styles; the director
picked take 1 of H03 by ear ("This one is it. So so cute"). DOWNFORCE ran eight lyric drafts and four rounds, 34 agent
takes in nine styles (darksynth, Italo, EBM darkwave pushed toward futurist synthwave, then faster at 140–150 BPM), and
the director picked none of them: they made their own take from the last draft ("lets use this one."), with a new
intro, two new pre-choruses and their own style. Every agent round's exclude list had begun "phonk, cowbell"; their
style begins "Memphis phonk, distorted cowbells". That is the third film whose final take the director made.
DON'T LET ME SCROLL is the fourth, and the first that started from one: the song came with the director's first message
("yo - i wrote this song:"), and a longer take "with better ending" followed. The film is cut to that
take, byte for byte. WHITEPILL is the fifth. Its production ran three rounds, 14 takes: a test lyric on three styles
(6), the first full draft merged from four written in parallel (4), and the second draft with its new hook (4). The
director picked none of them: the locked take is their own, of the second draft. THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE is the sixth. Its
production ran nine rounds and 54 takes over two songs: seven rounds on the first, whose locked take was the
production's, treated; and two on the second, a first lyric in two styles, then the rewritten lyric. The director's own
take of the rewrite is the song: "[…] this one!! so good […]". Keep every round's lyric, style and verdict in a takes log.

## Listening to it

Before anything is boarded, the song gets analysed: stems (htdemucs), a beat map, a start and end time for every word
(forced alignment), and a list of hits (kicks, snares, risers, drops) for the edit to land on.

- **Suno takes speed up.** ESCAPE VELOCITY went from 131.5 to 133.9 BPM and CLODYSSEY from 129 to 132: use a beat map,
  never a fixed BPM.
- **Put the grid on the kick.** BLISS's first beat grid sat on the hi-hats, 0.214 s early; every cut would have landed just
  before the beat.
- **Check word times by ear.** A delay throw got aligned as a word in ESCAPE VELOCITY, 2.6 s early.
- **Map a new mix before swapping it in.** An export from Suno's editor came back re-timed by up to 0.7 s; align it to the
  original by spectrogram first.
- **The take sings more than the sheet.** DOWNFORCE's countdown is twenty stuttered numbers before "Three, two, one",
  "point eight one" becomes eight "one"s, "done" repeats six times, and vocal chops run into the second verse. Forced
  alignment of the sheet failed there; the word times came from a transcription with word timestamps, the words from
  the director's sheet, and every stutter became a token on screen
  ([What to build](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/toolkit/#5-timed-words-and-the-ear-check)).
- **Check the tempo against the genre and the kick spacing.** DON'T LET ME SCROLL's first reading was 89 BPM. The
  style said 2-step, so the kick spacing was checked: the song runs at 133.39, and 89 had locked onto two-thirds of it.
  One constant grid then fits the whole song: a bar of 1.79922 s from 0.118 s, 90 bars.
- **A vocoder blurs the words.** After the last chorus Whisper heard "don't let me go" for "Don't let me scroll". Ask the
  writer: the production listed the words it heard with their times, and the director answered "yeah sounds right".
- **Take a singing shot's vocal from Suno's own stem split.** WHITEPILL's lead vocal, from Suno's split of the locked take
  (its Lead Vocal stem), matched the master to the millisecond, the same length with no lag; cut to each shot's window,
  it is what she sings to on camera ([Seedance](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/seedance/#lip-sync-from-the-songs-own-vocal)). Split the locked
  take itself, and check the stem against the master by cross-correlation (zero lag), not by title or length: the first
  stems came from the wrong takes ("thats teh wrong song"), one of them matched to the song by its length. The master
  minus 0.89 × that vocal is the music without her voice, for shots where she dances instead.
- **Check line starts against the vocal.** One reading of WHITEPILL's line starts was off by up to 0.5 s on 17 lines, and
  another began a line on its second word ("Rockets" is sung 0.6 s before "land"): the vocal stem's onsets settled each.
- **Follow the drift, 16th by 16th.** THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE's take runs from 95.7 to 99.4 BPM. The director: "oh yeah we
  have to be aware bpm slightly shifts w suno over the whole song so we may need to make sure the glitch timing is right
  with the drifting bpm." Its grid was tracked from the drums, every beat locked to its hit
  ([What to build](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/toolkit/#52-the-song-map-and-the-glitch-map)).
- **Map the voice where it is an instrument.** The song's own glitches (a frozen "lie" fluttering on 8ths, chopped vowels,
  a register drop and octave flips, a stutter) were found on spectrograms and pitch tracks and their hits measured from
  the stems, so the picture could glitch exactly on them. Word times from a transcription are good to about ±0.1 s there:
  cut on the bars or the glitch hits, never on word times.
- **Mark the song's own signature sounds.** AIRPLANE MODE's three download sections ("You wouldn't… download a car?")
  each sit between two bright chimes. The director: "theres this sweet sound effect bnefore and after the 'download'
  sections btw that we should hit". The first chime lands as a download starts and the second as it completes, each on
  its exact frame.
