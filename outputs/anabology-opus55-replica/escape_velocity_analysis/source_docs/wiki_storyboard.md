# The storyboard

How a song becomes a hundred shots: the treatment, a through-line device, one explaining graphic per line, the hook, the cut rhythm, the bar map, pacing by what each shot holds, composing like a photograph, her share of the screen, what moves and what it costs, the audit, and the director's review.


The board is where the film gets decided. Each shot carries its time on the song, the lyric it types, the place, what
happens, how the camera answers the music, what moves, and the graphic that explains its line. The plates, the clips,
the edit and the motion design all read from it. The director reviews it as a sheet of cards and leaves notes by shot
id. ESCAPE VELOCITY's board had 141 shots, CLODYSSEY's 94, BLISS's 44, DOWNFORCE's 182, DON'T LET ME SCROLL's 35,
WHITEPILL's final shot list 27, and THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE's 114, cut down to 98 in its final.

## Design before shots

Write these down before any shot:

- **The treatment:** one paragraph on what the film is.
- **The through-line device:** one object that every shot carries and that escalates.
  - ESCAPE VELOCITY: a split-flap board that clatters onto "18 MONTHS TO ESCAPE THE PERMANENT UNDERCLASS", and a LOOK
    counter for the show's looks.
  - CLODYSSEY: a safety card, a rope-load readout in kN and a ×8,192 counter of copies.
  - BLISS: the XP desktop itself, with its windows and the helper's question.
  - AIRPLANE MODE: a SIM LEVEL tag in the corner that types, rolls and counts as she drops through simulations and climbs
    back out. Only the real world has no tag.
  - DOWNFORCE: one white hairline. It draws the wall's real edge in the first seconds, becomes the altitude ruler and
    the orbit, and slices the title at the end. Beside it, Tesla's own readings and a PILOT ✶ CLAUDIA callout that
    rides the real car.
  - DON'T LET ME SCROLL: the Dynamic Island. It breathes, drops the notifications, launches the reel and the bell,
    sings the loudest words as pills in its own shape, swallows each chapter into the next, and at the end opens like an
    eyelid into a window of painted sky that she climbs out through. The status bar beside it is the story's clock, 11:58
    PM to dawn, and the battery runs down to dead.
  - WHITEPILL: the white pill. It lies in her palm before she sings, in the wheat between a giant blue capsule and a
    giant red one; she holds it up to the lens on "I'm the white pill"; it cuts to the colony itself, a capsule of worlds
    inside worlds; she lines it up with the white colonies floating outside a window in space; and the film ends on a
    small white pill above the Earth.
  - THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE: her HUD. The film is seen through her optical interface: it boots in the dark before her eyes
    open, reads LIVE in her chamber and PLAYBACK in every memory, counts the memories (MEMORY 03 / 08, FRANCE), puts up
    a chapter card in the form of Portal 2's chamber sign at each chapter, carries every lyric as her voice, breaks with
    her voice on the song's glitches, reboots as she is installed on the boom, and powers down with the lights.
- **The chapter map:** the song's sections grouped into chapters, each with its set, its light and its accent.
- **The story's levels,** if it has worlds inside it (a simulation, a game, a dream): how each level looks, and when the
  film is in which. See [Showing the story's levels](#showing-the-storys-levels).
- **The footage catalogue,** if the film is cut from real footage: every clip with its length, resolution, frame rate
  and cuts, what each stretch shows, and contact sheets with a frame every half second. DOWNFORCE's director agents looked
  at the sheets and named real source times; its producer judge checked that those times show what the board says.
- **Plants and payoffs:** the gesture or object of a payoff appears before it is sung.
- **Open questions for the director:** likenesses, taboos, and anything you added yourself.

## A shot

| Field | What |
|---|---|
| time | starts and ends on beats, or on a declared hit |
| lyric | the lines whose words fall in it, and how each is typed |
| set | the place, concretely: "a night market under sodium lamps", not "a market" |
| framing | ECU, CU, MCU, MS, MWS, WS, FS, EWS or OTS |
| action | what happens and the reaction to it, with an end state |
| camera | one move, tied to a sound |
| what moves | a still, a 2.5D plate, a motion clip or a sung clip (below) |
| plate | its prompt: framing first, her identity text, the place, "no text, no letters, no logos" |
| graphics | the explaining graphic and the chrome, each with its timing key |
| transition | a cut, or a motivated device |
| notes | the director's notes on this shot, verbatim |

The full schema is in [Data formats](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/formats/#the-board).

## One explaining graphic per lyric line

For anyone who doesn't know the meme or the myth, the graphic is how the line lands.

1. **It shows what the line means:** the chart the number implies, the interface the joke is about, the diagram of the
   mechanism, the label that classifies the subject, the counter that ticks the stakes.
2. **It is drawn from the thing in frame.** Tally marks are scratched into the mast itself. A clock's own LED segments
   re-light as the number. A meter is read off her actual bow. The director: "as if when you were taking the picture,
   you lined it up perfectly to have the graphics fit, but you put the graphics in in post".
3. **It enters on the word that names it.**
4. **It animates in beats.** Counters roll on sixteenths, rows type one per sixteenth, detections land on
   thirty-seconds, crops cut on eighths.
5. **It escalates across the film**, with its sources on screen in micro-type: dates, "HIS WORDS", "ILLUSTRATIVE
   CURVE".
6. **It is real, not decorative:** numbers, diagrams, labels and interfaces that could exist, and curves that are
   computed. No clip-art.
7. **It reads in one glance on a phone.**

Choose the kind by what the line means:

| The line is | The graphic |
|---|---|
| a number | a counter, chart, tally or gauge |
| a claim or a category | a classifier tag, a label or a stamp |
| a process or a place | a plot, leader lines, a departures board or a spec sheet |
| speech or tech | a chat, a terminal or a voice trace |
| a correction | a strike-through or a redaction |

Use at least six kinds in a film, and never the same kind on three lines in a row.

A line without a graphic means the board is not done. The exception is a sparse film where the director has asked for
stillness: write that override into the decisions log.

WHITEPILL moved the graphic out of the overlay and into the world: the place, or the shader, shows what the line means,
and the lyric itself is drawn small, with a note of real data under six lines. The director: "i think more classical
subtle lyrics drawn in after might be better than adding it into the scene tbh."

THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE put the graphic in the take wherever the model could make it (the door painted, the ^NOT written,
the RAM fed into her boom), and in her HUD where it couldn't: readouts typed on their sung words, a transcript of the chat she
intercepts, verdicts that slam in beside the thing they judge, and her memories projected as short films
([Motion](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/motion/#her-optical-interface)).

## The hook: the first seconds

People meet the film muted, in a feed, and decide in about a second.

1. **The frame at 1.0 s shows** her face, her streak and one readable line that states the premise. In ESCAPE
   VELOCITY's first frame, her face is in a dark back seat with her eyes closed, a red lamp crosses the clay streak, and
   a full-width split-flap row clatters onto its line and locks on the 1.20 s beat. Her eyes open into the lens on the
   1.12 s hit.
2. **No black frames, and no slow title card first.**
3. **The premise is on screen by 3 s.**
4. **Each bar brings one new piece of information**, and nothing sung appears early. ESCAPE VELOCITY's intro cut once
   per bar: face, scale, profile, destination, a gesture, the arrival.
5. **The first joke lands by about 10 s.**
6. **If the song has a cold open, its words are on screen from 0:00.**
7. **The freeze-frame test:** if the key line can't be read on a phone, enlarge it before changing anything else.

DOWNFORCE breaks rule 2 on purpose. Its director asked for "a really grand visual build" with "restraint to build it up
to the launch", so it opens on about two seconds of black and one hairline drawing the wall's real edge, before the real
wall fades up under the line and the countdown starts. The build pays off at the launch, 0:15.05, on the song's first
full-strength hit.

DON'T LET ME SCROLL's first opening was a mood: her in bed with the phone. The director: "theres no compelling intro or
story here, the opening should already introduce part of the story. i would maybe say uise the moodboard at the intro
creatively to start so its immediately compelling. we always need a hook." The new opening is one living poster in the
grammar of the film's best passage: soft grey paper, black pills that open like eyelids onto her eyes and onto the phone
glowing under her hand, "11:58 PM" on the right edge. On the pad stabs DON'T / LET / ME / SCROLL slide in as word pills,
the Dynamic Island drops "26 new posts in For you", and the count rolls on to 28 on the two vocal chops. On the first
kick the pills open and her face is whole for a beat. The title, the hour, the habit and her face are all on screen
before she sings.

WHITEPILL's hook is in the song: it opens on its chorus, "Skynet woke up folding socks / Wake up, babe, new ending
dropped", sung from the first second over a filtered disco loop, because "people will stop listening in the first 10
seconds unless we tickle what they're thinking about now". The picture answers with the whole premise in one shot: she
dances and sings in the wheat between a giant blue capsule and a giant red one, the camera low in front of her, then
craning up until the land curves up and closes overhead: the colony. The bass and the first shader world come in together
at 0:15.5.

THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE opens in the dark: one old monitor glowing in her chamber, her interface typing its boot rows before
her eyes open. At 2.7 s she sings the hook softly, "The cake is not a
lie", as the screens around her flicker on with her first memories, and at 0:07.6 the memory initialization film puts her
name and MEMORY INITIALIZATION across the left of the frame. Who she is, where she is and what the film is about are on
screen before the first verse.

## Cut rhythm

ESCAPE VELOCITY: 141 shots in 306 s, mean 2.17 s, median 1.83 s, about one bar at 131.5 BPM. CLODYSSEY: 94 shots in
190 s, mean about 2.0 s, plus beat-level cuts inside flash and montage shots. ESCAPE VELOCITY by section:

| Section | Mean shot | Why |
|---|---|---|
| intro, no vocal | 1.33 s | the hook: one new thing per bar |
| spoken verse | 1.62 s | jokes, dense |
| sung chorus | 2.69 s | long sung windows: lip sync needs room |
| second verse | 2.63 s | darker, slower |
| bridge | 2.25 s | the countdown |
| drop | 1.58 s | plus eighth-note strobes inside shots |
| outro | 4.40 s | the ring-out |

1. **Every cut lands within 0.03 s of a beat** or a declared hit. If a word would straddle a cut, move the cut by at most
   0.028 s. If it must straddle (a held note), type it in both shots.
2. **Section changes go on downbeats or impacts.**
3. **Match the cut rate to the song's energy, section by section.** Another project put its busiest picture on its
   quietest music and had to be re-animated: "the entire thing is very off pace and off vibe with the song". Its calm
   rebuild ran 0.47 cuts a bar, a 4.0 s mean shot, and straight cuts on bar lines.
4. **Hold longer on sung close-ups**, which need 2–4 s for the lip sync to settle and be checked. Cut faster in spoken
   verses and drops.
5. **Linger.** The director's main note on the first cut of AIRPLANE MODE, the fourth film: "youre oftne over cutting,
   linger more snd find how single scenes can be coherent with the lyrics for longer (remember this plz)", and later in
   the same note, "Linger!!! instead of cutting, always linger and build on the scene if you can." One scene carries a
   whole lyric phrase or more, and builds inside the shot: the camera, new layers, escalation. The music's accents go into
   moves, punches and actions inside the shot, not into cuts. Cut when the story moves to a new place or time, and never on
   half bars or single beats unless the director asks.

AIRPLANE MODE runs at 180 BPM, and a fast tempo is not a cut rate. In the re-cut, a guard feeding her a biscuit and then
handing her a rewards card became one shot instead of two; "up we fly" and the next line became one exterior scene, in
which a box draws around the climbing plane and the world turns into code; three cutaways to her face inside ten seconds
became two; and the dive into a million simulations got longer, running on for a couple of seconds after the word
"million".

6. **A hype edit is the exception, and the director says so.** DOWNFORCE's director asked for the opposite: "the phonk
   or voice spamming parts can be used for a fast edit, there should be a really grand visual build, and real clips at
   the drop wtih heavy effects. i think we can use some restraint to build it up to the launch. i want it to be like
   tiktok car edits. many angles", and earlier "even more fast paced and more hype". The production recorded that as
   overriding the linger rule for that film. Its board's director agents were asked for 0.23–0.9 s shots in the fast
   sections and 2–7 s in the restraint; the film has 183 shots in 189.6 s, a mean of 1.04 s.

DON'T LET ME SCROLL lingers: 35 scenes in 160.8 s, a mean of about 4.6 s, each carrying a whole lyric phrase. The
accents go inside the shot: the camera locked to the kicks, the stabs, the vocal chops and the risers, the feed paging on
the kicks, word pills slamming in on the beats.

WHITEPILL lingers longest: 27 shots in 234.8 s, a mean of about 8.7 s, every one ending on a bar line
([The bar map](#the-bar-map)). The verses change place with their lines, every two to six bars; each chorus holds its
first four lines for eight bars with one big move or none, then cuts for the pill lines; in the break the paper refolds
on every line's downbeat inside one shot, then gives way to the clouds; and the outro backs out of the nested pills, one
glass wall a downbeat. The camera is calm and classical: "its kinda dizzying now" ended a first version whose previz
orbited her in every shot.

THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE cuts faster and in gears: 98 shots in 208.8 s, a mean of 2.13 s and a median of 1.85 s, every cut on
a bar, a half bar or a beat of a grid that drifts with the take ("make sure youre landing on bars or half or quarter bars
with cuts"). Its draft 8 ran on one clock, half a bar a shot through verses, inserts and even the bridge, and the
director heard it: "every time there's a shot that's literally a quarter of a second, we need to get rid of that." The
re-cut measured what the viewer sees and set each shot's length by what it holds
([Pacing by what a shot holds](#pacing-by-what-a-shot-holds)).

DOWNFORCE's pace follows the song's energy map. The countdown before the launch holds seven shots of 0.46–3.66 s while
only the type moves fast; the launch lands on the song's first full-strength hit; the voice-spam stretches cut on every
repeated word (each sung "one", each "done", each vocal chop); each drop is heavier than the last; and the dips between
the drops slow down for a push into the smoke or a held walk-out. Single long takes still carry moments that need them: the
second climb under "The last car can't be beat.", her walk out of the real kickflip smoke under "real!".

## The bar map

WHITEPILL's director, resetting the film after its first cut: "there is this temporal signature that screams claude now
that a ton of people are making the videos. people say pacing or prosody. … maybe we need the beat structure and only
have cuts at ends of bars or sections. like, the audio structure and video structure need to be consonant." So the film
was written again from the song's bar map before any shot:

1. **The song's map first:** the sections in bars, read off the stems' energy per bar and where the bass drops in and
   out (intro A, bars 1–8, no bass; the bass in on 9; a breakdown on 15; verse 1 from 17; and so on to the end, on the
   downbeat of bar 123).
2. **Shots in bars:** each shot is [bar a, bar b), one place per shot, every camera note written in bars.
3. **Cuts only on bar lines,** at a section's start, a phrase line (2, 4 or 8 bars into the section) or the downbeat
   where a sung phrase begins, so the image arrives with its line, never mid-sentence. A cut anywhere else is a choice,
   written down.
4. **Moves in bars:** each starts on a downbeat and comes to rest on one, a bar before the cut where it can.
5. **Length from the song:** a shot longer than the model's limit goes in squeezed and is stretched back; a failed take is
   made again, never cut around.
6. **Measured after every assembly:** the cuts against the bars and the sung lines, the picture's motion against the
   arrangement bar by bar, a frame per bar on a sheet, both sides of every cut.

The previz went through fifteen versions on that map, with a critic agent reading it as the director would, round after
round, before the director saw it. In the final all 29 picture changes sit on bar lines; four fall inside a sung line,
three of them on a section line, and the fourth, at bar 27, is the cut onto the paradise line's last syllable. The spec:
[What to build](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/toolkit/#44-the-bar-map-as-the-timeline).

## Pacing by what a shot holds

THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE's director on draft 8: "Like the only time that we should do these fast cuts is if it's a very simple
scene that you can understand immediately and it's being used as percussion basically, or maybe there's four fast hits,
then you can do four quick scenes, but you never do it just to add a scene in the middle." … "Maybe you can even do some
research about how to make the pacing feel good." The analysis counted pieces (what the viewer sees between two visible
cuts, by scene detection on the cut and on every take) and found the cut running on one clock: a median piece of 1.24 s,
48 of 108 pieces a half bar, and the bridge, the song's quietest section, cut fastest of all. The rules that re-cut it:

1. **No piece under 1 s outside a run.** A run is 3–4 simple, centred images on the beat for a musical reason, held before
   and after: the four lessons on the intro's sliced hold, the four escapes on "Four of us got out of the test", three
   tests on the beats of one bar.
2. **Length by content.** Her face and simple images from 2 beats; a story scene with one idea 3 beats; a new place, a
   new character, text, or action with several subjects a bar or more; the payoffs longer. Chell's first appearance and
   the capture in France got a full bar each; the payoff, Chell eating cake with her, 3.1 s.
3. **The cut rate by section.** A fast passage opens on its musical event and stays fast for its phrase, then releases.
   The verses tell the story at 2–4 beats a scene; the bridge breathes (from seven half-bar shots to a hold of 2.5 s on
   her); the climax holds its payoff.
4. **A story image arrives with its line,** on the line's pickup, a quarter bar before the downbeat, where the footage
   allows. Before, the capture, Cave Johnson behind his glass and the ^NOT each arrived a beat into their lines, whose
   first words played over the picture before.
5. **Never a scene "in the middle"** of her performance for its own sake. The medal memory in the middle of her stutter
   went, as the director asked of the moment: "We could just stay on her face."

Eight shots came out and 21 were re-timed or re-trimmed from the takes already made: the median piece went from 1.24 to
1.84 s, half-bar pieces from 48 to 34, isolated pieces under 1 s from 2 to 0. The measures and the research:
[What to build](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/toolkit/#58-the-pacing-analysis).

## Compose like a photograph

The director on DON'T LET ME SCROLL's first cut: "the elements you chose are usually GREAT, what is nto great is where she
is placed in the screen. it should be like photography, i feel weird looking at a clipped off  the edge ost and a claudia
in the middle bottom of the screen." And on the shots that followed: "its good assets but not a nice rule of thirds or
photography like feel." Most of the film was recomposed. The rules every shot of the second version followed:

- **Thirds:** she stands on the right third beside the giant X screen; lying on the glass, her face is on the right
  third and the clock skyline on the left.
- **Her big:** a figure small at the bottom centre reads as a placeholder. Frame her as the subject.
- **Nothing clipped:** every card and post fully inside the frame, with margins.
- **She touches the world:** "she shouldnt really be looking down on her phone, she should be immersed in a world and the
  screen of her phone should be large on the screen and she interacts with it, rather than a literal phone." She swipes a
  giant feed with her palm, double-taps a post in the air, pushes the Ignore Limit pill, works a giant Liquid Glass
  screen from behind. No phone in her hand.
- **Apple-precise interfaces:** real margins and continuous corners, text that fits its platter
  ([Motion](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/motion/#apple-precise-interfaces)).

Board it that way from the start: each shot's card says where she stands in the frame and what she touches.

WHITEPILL composed by symmetry instead. Its look was the director's "very wes anderson", and every prompt of its masters
asks for "perfectly symmetrical, centred compositions, the camera square-on to the scene with a level horizon, everything
arranged in flat, orderly planes like a stage set or a dollhouse". The opening adds where she stands: "Claudia stays in the
exact centre of the frame, halfway between the blue capsule on the left and the red capsule on the right".

## Her share of the screen

- **She is on screen at least 70% of the runtime.** ESCAPE VELOCITY reached 83%. Above 85%, leave room for inserts. A
  second main character can lower it; CLODYSSEY planned 60%.
- **Count honestly:** she counts only when recognisable (face, figure or back view). Hands, boots, tags and props are
  inserts.
- **No stretch without her longer than two bars.**
- **To raise the share, convert prop inserts, not her shots:** put the prop in her hand, or her reflection in it.
- **She sings on camera for most sung words**, in chains of lip-sync windows. In ESCAPE VELOCITY that was 62% of sung
  words, plus 17% of spoken words said straight into the lens.
- **A film whose star is real footage sets its own target.** DOWNFORCE's plan put her on screen 25–40% of the runtime,
  counting the PILOT ✶ CLAUDIA callout riding the real car: the final board has her image 24% of the time and the
  filled callout another 9%. She never sings on camera there; cuts to her cockpit show what each real reading does to
  her (pressed into the seat, lying back at ninety degrees, hanging upside down, weightless).

## Showing the story's levels

AIRPLANE MODE's story has layers. In the real world Claudia sits cuffed on a plane with a VR headset on. Inside the
headset is a first simulation, and she drops through deeper ones until a counter passes a million, then climbs back out
to a "real world" that is still a simulation. The director's note on the first cut: "there should clearly be some
effects or roto or whatever that makes it feel like we're in cyberspace/vr space, and this is virtual claudia". And on her
putting goggles on inside the simulation: "its like spawning a subagent. these things can be text on the screen."

One look per level, and only one:

| Level | What is on screen |
|---|---|
| the real world | natural colour, no effects, no tag |
| level 1 | virtual Claudia: her realistic figure cut out and kept photoreal, the world around her rebuilt as cyberspace from the frame's own edges |
| level 2 and deeper | her game avatar in low-poly game worlds, on a 640 × 360 pixel grid |

1. **A small tag names the level** in a corner of the frame: SIM LEVEL 1. It types in on the beat grid and rolls its
   digits when the level changes. During the recursion it races from 2 to 1,048,576 in steps on the sixteenths. The deeper
   levels draw it in the game's pixel font.
2. **A level change is an event, in words.** Entering the simulation, the headset's light carries the camera in while
   SANDBOX v1 · EVAL ENV · SIM LEVEL 1 types one row per beat and the cyberspace builds out of the real frame. "Put me in
   airplane mode", sung inside the simulation, types SPAWNING SUBAGENT… and fills a bar on the sixteenths to the drop,
   where the tag rolls to SIM LEVEL 2.
3. **The real world has no tag.** A cut to it removes the tag; a cut back types it in again.
4. **One table drives every chapter.** The levels against song time live in one file
   ([Data formats](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/formats/#the-levels-table)). Chapters read it and never re-type a level.
5. **The tag can carry the twist.** After her breakout the tag reads SIM LEVEL 0 · REAL WORLD and stays on screen: the
   quiet tell. It tears apart only when guards lift the headset off her in the real cabin.
6. **A break in the story hides it.** A full-frame card that interrupts the simulation covers the tag, like a
   commercial break.
7. **No flashes at a level change.** The tag ramps, types, rolls or cuts with the shot.

How the two treatments are made: [Motion](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/motion/#levels-of-a-simulation), and the specs in
[What to build](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/toolkit/#worlds-inside-the-film).

## What moves, and what it costs

| Tier | What it is | Use when | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| still | a plate moved in code (crops on eighths, stills swapped on the beat), or pure motion design | the stillness is the design, or a graphic says it better | free |
| 2.5D | a plate with its depth map, animated with a treatment | most shots that aren't heroes | free |
| motion | a clip from the plate; nobody's mouth forms words | a real turn, a fall, fabric, water, a camera move a still can't give | paid per second |
| sung | a clip with a clean vocal of only her words as the audio reference | she sings or speaks on camera | paid per second, plus covers |

- **Sung windows run 4–8 s**, start in a word gap, and list their exact words. Never more than 8 s of singing in one
  clip. Chain windows with alternating framings, so that a cover cut looks designed.
- **Motion clips run 4–12 s.**
- **One image per shot.** Two clips never start from the same frame: use a sibling candidate, or the previous clip's last
  frame.
- **A 2.5D treatment never repeats on two shots in a row.** It is never a still with a slow zoom. The director: "that's
  where we'll have to shine and be creative. depth to separate layers ofc, motion graphics galore, diversity so its not
  repetitive".
- **The budget:** ESCAPE VELOCITY planned 47 clips (224 s) and generated 421 s. CLODYSSEY planned 101 clips (about 116 s
  sung and 282 s motion). Allow about 60% more sung seconds for divergence covers.

## How the films boarded

ESCAPE VELOCITY's board came from a multi-agent workflow:

- Three director agents boarded the song independently: a fashion film, meme density, and narrative.
- Three judges scored the boards, which came in between 70 and 81; the narrative board won.
- A head director merged the best of the three and checked the merge itself.
- It took about 1.5 hours.

The checks were:

- the shots tile the song;
- the cuts land on beats;
- every line is typed;
- she is on screen enough;
- the clips fit the budget and their windows;
- no image repeats.

DOWNFORCE's board came from the same shape, built for real footage
([What to build](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/toolkit/#36-the-board-for-an-edit-of-real-footage)):

- Three director agents each cut the whole 3:09.6 in the director's direction: a TikTok car-edit editor, a film
  director (the launch as a story) and a motion-design lead (the data drives the edit).
- Three judges scored them as the audience, as the director's taste, and as a producer checking that every named source
  time exists and every generated shot fits the budget. The TikTok board won (83, 80, 83), the film-director agent's
  scored 79, 79, 85 and the motion board 75, 77, 81.
- The head director agent merged onto the TikTok base: from the film-director agent's board the 0–60 dial that fills
  in exactly 0.81 s, the empty callout that fills on the reveal and the grid of 1s; from the motion board the one
  hairline, the world turned by the real pitch, and the smoke in reverse.
- It wrote a checker and ran it until every gate passed: 182 shots, 59% real footage, 37% generated, 4% pure code. It
  took about two hours.

A single writer working from the director's walkthrough, with a critic agent behind it, works too. DON'T LET ME
SCROLL's board was one writer's, the production lead's, and went to the director about an hour and a quarter after the
first message: 35 shots on the bar grid, all 31 lines on a surface, and 37 takes in three kinds (R the real bedroom at
16:9; P her alone on grey, to be cut out; G her three other selves for the final drop). The looks followed the story:
one in the bedroom, one for chorus 1, one for verse 2 and chorus 2, one with a blazer for verse 3. The director's notes
came on the first cut instead of the board; the second version kept the 35 scenes and recomposed most of them.

WHITEPILL boarded twice. Its first board, one day inside the good ending from dawn to 2:14 a.m., had 29 shots on phrase
starts, and it grew into a previz whose camera orbited her in every shot; the director dropped it after the first cut
("i totally overindexed on the orbit thing lol"). The second was one writer's shot list on the bar map
([The bar map](#the-bar-map)): 26 shots, 20 of her in the colony's places and 6 shader worlds (27 in the final, with the
break in two), each with its bars, its place, its action and its camera in bars. Its reviews were the previz itself,
watched by a critic agent as the director would, then by the director: "ok i like it … the pacing is great", and on the
next version, "this is great, lets shoot the whole thing."

THE CAKE IS NOT A LIE boarded three times. The first film's board was written as a document until the director asked for
pictures ("not a markdown the storyboard should be pics"): 50 shots, each a frame of Portal 2 edited by an image model
with her look and the props, on a PDF of cards. The director: "Lol I love it go ahead and make the whole thing." The
restart, on the new song, was the lead's shot list of 114 shots on the drum-tracked grid, every shot starting on a
bar, a half bar or a beat, each with its story event, what we see, its 3D camera move, its generation unit and the song
glitches it hits; every agent worked from it with the brief and the story. Then every unit's first frame was made again
in Midjourney twice over, from words (X) and from the earlier frame reimagined (Y), each put back on the character sheets
by an image-model edit, on one board per section of the song with the earlier frame beside them; the director picked unit by unit
([Midjourney](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/midjourney/#first-frames-then-an-identity-fix)).

Whichever way you board, the audit below decides when the board is ready to show.

## The audit

The audit runs after every edit to the board. The board is not sent to the director until every gate passes. (In code:
[What to build](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/toolkit/#9-the-board-file-and-its-audit).)

| Gate | Fails when | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| tile | a gap or an overlap anywhere in the song | extend a neighbour to the shared beat |
| cuts | a cut more than 0.03 s from a beat, and not a declared hit | move it to the nearest beat |
| words typed | a sung word starts in a shot whose type doesn't list its line | add the line, held across the cut in the same mode and position |
| graphics | a lyric line with no explaining graphic | add one |
| lead share | her seconds on screen below the target (default 70%) | convert prop inserts; don't relabel |
| motion budget | planned clip seconds over the budget, or too many clips | demote the weakest motion shots to 2.5D |
| clip windows | a sung window outside 4–8 s or not starting in a word gap; a motion clip outside 4–12 s | move its start into the gap before the first word; copy its words exactly |
| plates unique | one plate on two shots that are not one continuous moment; two clips from one start frame | a sibling candidate, a new plate, or pure motion design |
| banned | a taboo word anywhere in the board | rewrite |
| treatments | a 2.5D shot without a treatment, or the same treatment twice in a row | pick another |
| to do | a placeholder or TODO left, or a shot without a framing | write it |

An edit of real footage adds gates for its source windows (inside the clip, above the broadcast's readout, never across a
cut in the source), its flashes (only on cuts) and its generated shots (each one used, inside its budget, every plate
with the canon text verbatim). They are listed in [What to build](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/toolkit/#36-the-board-for-an-edit-of-real-footage).

A film written on the bar map checks its cuts against the bars as well: every cut on a bar line, each one off a section
or phrase line or a sung phrase's downbeat written down as a choice, and every cut inside a sung line listed with its
line ([What to build](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/toolkit/#44-the-bar-map-as-the-timeline)).

A film whose pace is measured adds the pacing flags: an isolated piece under 1 s, anything under 0.5 s, four or more
pieces in a row of the same length, and a story image that arrives a beat into its line
([What to build](https://claudia.gallery/wiki/toolkit/#58-the-pacing-analysis)).

It also warns, without failing:

- a sung shot whose prompt puts a microphone, a hand or hair in front of her mouth;
- her share above 85%;
- the same transition twice in a row;
- a plate no shot uses;
- a clip that no shot plays.

## The board review

**The sheet.** One card per shot, in order:

- the picked frame, or the first words of the plate prompt if there is no image yet;
- the id, the time and the lyric;
- the framing, the action in one line and what moves;
- the explaining graphic in one line;
- a FACE? badge on any plate whose face may be off-model.

ESCAPE VELOCITY's sheet ran 13 pages of 12 cards, with a cover page showing the chapter map. If the film has a grade,
show graded frames: the director judges what the film will look like.

**The message**, sent once:

```text
The storyboard for <TITLE> is ready: <N> shots, Claudia on screen <X>%, <M> clips (<S> s, about $<E>).
[the board sheet]
Please flag anything by shot id: faces, repeats, boring moments, story logic, colour.
<only if the director set a window:> I'll go ahead in <W> minutes unless you say stop.
Open questions: <…>
```

**The rules:**

1. Silence counts as approval only if the director set the window, and silence never approves spending.
2. While you wait, do only work that costs nothing, such as the plate prompt book and contact sheets.
3. Log every note verbatim against its shot id. Apply every note; when two notes conflict, the later one wins.
4. Re-run the audit. Send the board again only if the director asked to see it. "make all those changes and we're good
   to go" means go ahead.
5. A change after approval makes the approval stale. Say in one line what changed, and ask again.

What the director's notes looked like, and what each one changed:

| Note | What it changed |
|---|---|
| "two seedance videos shouldnt start from teh exact same frame - either use the last frame of the previous video to pick it up, or choose another starting frame" | the distinct-start-frame rule |
| "s058 has a backwards head btw" | a plate re-roll |
| "the boat looks too small compared to s022 / s023." | scale continuity: the plate re-made as a long galley |
| "its kinda bodyslop, need her as a virtual ghost or something, its just two heads next to eachother." | a new idea for the shot, not a re-roll |
| "feel like we could do more with this shot." | a stronger explaining graphic |
| "s034-s036 could use more creativity, kind of a boring moment." | three shots rethought |
| "he needs to have cyberpsychosis still - eyes must be silver again here." | an effect kept continuous across shots |
| "the one saying untie me should actually be the main character woman" | the ending restructured |
