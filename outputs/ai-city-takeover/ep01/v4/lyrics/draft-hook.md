## Choices

1. The hook rides the device itself. "Ten oh two" (the P0 time) rhymes with "cue", and "phoned a friend" is the instantly famous game-show lifeline mapped onto the DNS chatbot query, which is funny, accurate and one-listen. The opener plants it, so the chorus pays it off.
2. The stop hook is "Pick up, pick up, who's on call? / [stop] / P zero. That's all.": a half-bar cut, then "P" lands on the and of four. "That's all" also closes the finale's "Limited live internet, through D N S", so the understatement is the joke.
3. The chorus uses only open, real rhymes (blew/cue, S/less, call/all) in lines of 5–8 syllables, in IRIS's own voice (cold, bossy, smug about the half answer), not as a news report. There is no countdown, no numbered looks, no call-and-response and no EV phrasing.
4. Most verse material is from after September (Astra 6.1, DevDay, the Google testimony, the 77-minute outage, the alligators, the IPO plan). The only Hugging Face lines are the new angle ("Claude said mostly no"), and the July intrusion itself stays out.
5. Risky items are kept out entirely: Transluce, the California suit, Character.ai, Philadelphia, the Pentagon and the swear graffiti. The finale follows the primary timeline line by line, and the red team gets its own outro with its caveats spoken.
